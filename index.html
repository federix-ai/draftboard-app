On your phone · with Tailscale

Your phone, set up in five steps.

Your Mac serves the page and only your own devices reach it, through a free Tailscale account. Nothing goes on the internet and nothing passes through us. About five minutes, once.

  1. 1

    Install Tailscale on both

    On the Mac and on the iPhone, from tailscale.com/download or the App Store. Sign in with the same account on both: the personal plan is free.

  2. 2

    Turn it on in Bashestra

    In Settings › Notifications › In the phone's browser, switch on Open Bashestra from the phone. Bashestra finds Tailscale and tells you what's missing, if anything.

  3. 3

    Turn on HTTPS certificates

    If it says HTTPS is off, click Open the console: on the DNS page turn on HTTPS Certificates (MagicDNS stays on), then Retry. Ready shows the address, your-mac.….ts.net, and a QR code.

  4. 4

    Pair the phone

    Scan the QR with the iPhone's camera and open the page in Safari. On the Mac click Pair a phone and type the 6-digit code into the page within 10 minutes.

  5. 5

    Put it on the Home Screen

    In Safari: Share › Add to Home Screen. Open it from the icon and pair once more: the icon and Safari keep separate cookies, so each one pairs once.

If Bashestra says…

Tailscale is not installedInstall it on the Mac and the phone: Download Tailscale opens the page. It's free for personal use.
Tailscale is not connectedOpen Tailscale on the Mac and sign in with your account, then Retry.
HTTPS is off in your Tailscale networkOpen the console, DNS page: turn on HTTPS Certificates, then Retry. The very first visit can take a few seconds while the certificate is made.
Funnel is on for this MacFunnel would put the page on the internet, so Bashestra won't start it. Turn Funnel off and Retry.
Port 443 of serve is already in useAnother program uses tailscale serve on this Mac and Bashestra won't take it over. Free it, then Retry.
The page asks for a code againIt's a browser that isn't paired yet: the Home Screen icon, Safari and Telegram's own browser each pair once.

Only yours

  • Bashestra listens only inside the Mac (127.0.0.1). The phone gets there through tailscale serve, encrypted, and only from your own devices.
  • Every browser pairs with a code and the Mac keeps only a hash of its secret. Revoke one, or all, from the same settings.
  • From the phone you never approve “Always”, and each button decides only the request it shows.
  • Bashestra must be open and the Mac awake: with the lid closed, the Mac sleeps.

Using Telegram too? Its messages get an Open button that leads to the page: have Telegram open links in Safari, so you're already paired. Telegram is set up in Settings › Notifications › On your phone, with a bot you create with @BotFather.

The tour

Six things you'll do on day one.

The real screens of the app. Scroll and it plays, chapter by chapter.

  1. 01

    Launch a team

    Press ⇧⌘T (or + New › Team), pick a team and write the task. The agents appear on the canvas already linked, each in its own worktree; the leader splits the work.

  2. 02

    Linked agents talk

    Draw a line between two nodes, or hold on a node and pick from the wheel: write, link, loop, hand off. Inside the terminal agents use orch send; if a colleague is busy, the message waits in its queue, and you can reorder or move it.

  3. 03

    Step in only when needed

    A permission request turns the node orange and you get a notification. The capsule in the bar says who is waiting: ⌘J takes you there, one click and it carries on.

  4. 04

    A worktree per agent

    In a git repo every agent works on its own orch/<name> branch, so nobody steps on anyone. The Git section of the strip shows the graph, diffs, commit and push.

  5. 05

    Floors for experiments

    Clone the whole workspace and its repos instantly with APFS. Try an idea on a floor, then bring the branches back, or throw it away.

  6. 06

    Point at the bug

    Web and iOS Simulator nodes sit next to the agents. Capture the screen, circle what's wrong, add a note and send it to the linked agent.

And then some

Everything a conductor needs.

Bashestra doesn't replace your agents: it runs the CLIs you already use, with your own accounts, and gives them a score to play from.

The Maestro runs the canvas

Make any session the Maestro: it reads the whole board, creates sessions and teams, links them and hands out tasks. Anything that starts a process waits for your Allow.

A model and effort per role

Opus to lead and review, Sonnet to write and test. Each role brings its skills and rules; change them per session from the Sessions section.

Nothing ships unreviewed

The Reviewer checks the whole diff at the end of every phase. Until the verdict arrives nobody gets new tasks; after three rejections, you decide. When the plan ends, the leader writes you a report.

One project, many folders

An iOS app and its Android twin on the same board, each with its own zone and colour. Finish a feature in one and the Porting team carries it to the other, as a branch.
